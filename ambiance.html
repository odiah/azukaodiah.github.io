Ambiance

People often describe environments in emotional terms — cozy, calm, inviting, productive, stressful. Despite how central these impressions are to everyday life, we still know surprisingly little about which design choices create them. My research asks:

  • What specific design elements make a space feel relaxing, productive, or creative?
  • Which features recur across different emotional environments?
  • How can we systematically characterize ambiance?
  • How do personality, culture, age, and context shape these experiences?

To study this, I build and analyze large datasets of environmental descriptions and design features — covering lighting, color, layout, materials, furniture, and spatial organization. One current project focuses on living rooms; a second extends this framework to workplaces, examining how office spaces convey productivity, focus, creativity, calm, and collaboration.

Methods: large language models, AI-generated environmental descriptions, statistical modeling, and computational feature extraction. By identifying patterns across thousands of descriptions, this work moves beyond intuition toward a systematic understanding of how spaces generate emotional experience — with the long-term goal of designing environments that better support well-being, productivity, and everyday psychological functioning.
