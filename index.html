Hi there!

How do the spaces we live in shape how we feel?

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Social and Personality Psychology at the University of Texas at Austin, working with Sam Gosling.

My work explores how architectural design elements interact with individual differences like personality, emotion, and cognition to regulate emotion and shape psychological experience. I use tools from behavioral science, statistics, large language models (LLMs), and generative AI to investigate how the physical environments we live in influence how people feel, think, and behave.

Influenced by Wole Soyinka’s philosophy of storytelling, I see spaces as narratives where I look at how design elements and sensory cues create meaning and guide lived experience.

I am open to opportunities for learning and collaboration. If you have any questions about my research, feel free to reach out to me.

02Research thread

Ambiance

Add header image:
assets/research-ambiance.jpg

People usually describe environments using emotional terms: “cozy,” “calm,” “inviting,” “creative,” “productive,” or “stressful.” But despite the importance of these experiences, there is still very little systematic research explaining what specific combinations of design features create different ambiances. My research asks questions like:

  • What specific design elements make a space feel relaxing or productive, etc.?
  • Which design features consistently appear in different emotional environments?
  • How can we systematically characterize ambiances?
  • How do personality, culture, age, and context shape these experiences?

To investigate this, I build and analyze large datasets of environmental descriptions and design features. One current project focuses on living rooms and examines how different ambiances are represented through combinations of:

  • lighting
  • color
  • layout
  • materials
  • furniture
  • objects and decoration
  • spatial organization

A second ongoing project applies this framework to workplace environments, examining how office spaces convey ambiances not limited to:

  • production
  • focus
  • creativity
  • calmness
  • collaboration
  • invitation

To study these questions, I use:

  • large language models
  • AI-Generated environmental descriptions
  • statistical modeling
  • computational feature extraction

By identifying recurring feature patterns across thousands of descriptions, this work aims to move beyond intuition and toward a more systematic understanding of how spaces generate emotional experience. The long-term goal is to help create environments that more intentionally support well-being, productivity, restoration, and everyday psychological functioning.

03Research thread

Personality and the Built Environment

Add header image:
assets/research-personality.jpg

People differ in the kinds of spaces they prefer: some enjoy bright, open, socially stimulating environments, while others prefer quieter, more structured, or more visually complex spaces. My work investigates how personality traits relate to preferences for features like:

  • lighting and windows
  • openness versus enclosure
  • density and privacy
  • color palettes
  • furniture and décor
  • architectural style
  • organization and clutter
  • walkability and urban form

To study this, I conducted an interdisciplinary review spanning psychology, environmental psychology, architecture, interior design, and urban studies. Across this literature, emerging patterns suggest that:

  • Extraversion is associated with brighter, more open, socially stimulating spaces
  • Conscientiousness relates to orderly, structured environments
  • Openness to Experience links to novelty, abstraction, and visual complexity
  • Neuroticism connects to sensitivity to stressors like noise, density, and lack of privacy

The broader goal of this work is to better understand person-environment fit: how different environments may support different psychological needs and ways of functioning.
